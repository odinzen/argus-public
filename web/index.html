argus-citations

Check a bibliography against the Crossref record for every work.

Paste a reference list (BibTeX, CSL-JSON, or a plain formatted list copied from a document) and press Check, or load a PDF or Word manuscript. Your file stays in your browser; only DOIs (or search terms) are sent to Crossref.

Select multiple files, a whole folder, or drag them onto this box. BibTeX/CSL-JSON give the exact check (full author list, initials, title, year, volume, first page). PDF/Word is a best-effort audit: the reference list is extracted as text and each entry matched to Crossref.

The email is optional: adding it routes your lookups through Crossref's faster, more reliable lane (their "polite pool"). Leave it blank to stay anonymous. Nothing but the DOIs, and that email if you give one, leaves your browser.

What a flag means

A flagged reference is a prompt to look, not an accusation of misconduct. A mismatch usually sits somewhere on this spectrum:

Clerical error A real paper with a mistyped detail: a wrong year, volume, or author initial. Ordinary human slips.
Distorted citation A real paper carrying details that belong to another: swapped co-authors, a reworded title. This is the most common machine-assisted failure, not invention but distortion of a real record.
Hallucinated reference A citation to a work that does not appear to exist at all.

Argus shows which references disagree with the authoritative record so a person can check them. It never edits a citation, fills a field from a model, or infers intent.

Why it matters

Distorted and non-existent references have risen sharply in the literature alongside AI-assisted writing, and most are not whole inventions but real papers with corrupted details, the kind a resolving DOI or a first-author glance slips past. Argus is a small, auditable check against the canonical record, with no language model in the verification path.
